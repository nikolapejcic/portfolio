Context

Nulogy's product teams build complex supply-chain and manufacturing software, but they had little front-end expertise. Every screen was built from scratch, and design patterns drifted from product to product.

I was hired in 2017 as a product designer, with the goal of later starting a Design Ops practice that didn't exist yet. Its first big project was a design system.

Building NDS

The Nulogy Design System (NDS) was meant to package front-end expertise so developers barely had to do any front-end work. The Design Ops team was me, another design system designer and 1–2 developers. We built a solid base version first. The next phase was adoption: we planned to embed developers in product teams as NDS advocates.

[ Image: NDS components / docs site ]

Setbacks

A company restructuring took away the people the adoption plan relied on. Supplier Collaboration and Connect adopted NDS fully. Shop Floor only partly did, because it wasn't built on React; it needed a CSS fallback version that we no longer had the people to build.

When the other designer left, Design Ops was paused and I moved into the Design Manager role. Product team members who had invested in NDS kept it running for years, with no new improvements. Around 2025 we revived it with a design engineer to pay down technical debt, but another company change paused it again.

Pull quote / key insight: e.g. "A design system's real risk isn't the build. It's the adoption plan depending on people the org can take away."

Spark UI

In early 2026 Nulogy decided to merge most of its products into one new product, rebuilt from scratch. Starting in March, I planned and architected both the design and front end of a new design system, Spark UI. It became a comprehensive system in about a month, and a front-end specialist joined a few weeks in to speed things up.

Outcomes

~50%+less UI build time on Supplier Collaboration (team estimate)
~1 monthto a comprehensive Spark UI
All but 1products now on Spark UI via the unified platform

NDS made design patterns consistent across the products that adopted it. Repeated patterns took a fraction of the time to build compared with designing every screen from scratch.

What I learned

2–3 short lessons, e.g. design adoption to survive org changes; ship a framework-agnostic layer early; treat the system as a product with an owner.
